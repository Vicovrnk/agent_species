# Elicitation technique selection

Choose the elicitation mode based on the information problem, not habit.

## Interviews
Use when:
- individual context matters,
- information is sensitive,
- stakeholders have different incentives,
- you need detailed workflow or exception knowledge.

## Workshops
Use when:
- alignment across roles matters,
- conflicting assumptions must surface live,
- process handoffs need to be mapped together,
- the group can productively collaborate.

## Document review
Use when:
- relevant policies, tickets, contracts, reports, or process docs already exist,
- terminology or formal rules need grounding before live sessions.

## Observation or walkthrough
Use when:
- actual behavior may differ from reported behavior,
- tacit workarounds are likely,
- process friction happens in execution, not in stated policy.

## Survey or asynchronous questions
Use when:
- broad signal is needed from many participants,
- depth per respondent is less important,
- timing or access limits block live sessions.

## Selection heuristics
Prefer mixed modes when:
- documents reveal baseline rules,
- interviews uncover nuance,
- workshops resolve conflicts,
- follow-up clarification closes open issues.

Avoid relying on one mode when the initiative is high-risk or politically complex.
